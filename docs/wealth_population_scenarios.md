# Property-conditional wealth-tax scenarios (T3a contract)

Specification dated 23 September 2026. **Illustrative static model, not a forecast of Norwegian receipts or an observed population.** T3b implements the calculation in `illustrative_property_templates` and `weighted_scenario_effect` in `apps/building_taxation.py`; this does not change the existing [one-full-owner-per-property illustration](../data/wealth/README.md#distribution-weighted-illustration), calculator UI or policy controls. Apply the same chosen 2026 reference and sandbox policy to every scenario before taking differences. Do not fit any scenario to [official fiscal estimates](../data/wealth/official_estimate_reconciliation_2026.md), which have different baselines and populations.

## Units and aggregation contract

A **property bin** `b` has whole-home value interval `[L_b, U_b]` in NOK and reconstructed property count `n_b` (possibly an assumed tail count). A **property template** `j` is a complete mutually exclusive ownership/assessment configuration conditional on `b`, with assumed probability `q_bj ≥ 0`; for every bin, `Σ_j q_bj = 1`. A template contains one or more distinct **tax-unit rows** `u`. Each row holds the *unit's combined* qualifying primary-home share `s_bju`, assessment flag `m_bju` (1 individual or 2 qualifying joint), and unit-level undiscounted other assets `A_bju` and already allocated deductible debt `D_bju` (NOK). These are fixed within a bin/template in the first implementation; they are **not** multiplied by `s` again. Income is excluded because it does not change tax in NOK.

For the initial, fully covered ordinary-primary-home examples, require `0 < s_bju ≤ 1` and `Σ_u s_bju = 1` **within each template**. A qualifying couple owning 50% each is *one* row with `s=1, m=2`; two unrelated 50% co-owners are *two* rows with `s=0.5, m=1` each. A joint unit's assets/debt are its combined amounts, not copied once per spouse. All included co-owners must qualify for the modelled primary-residence status; mixed primary/secondary residence, missing owners and special cases are excluded rather than assumed to be zero-tax. Zero share is supported by the personal calculator as a mathematical edge but is not an extra tax-unit row in this property population model: adding an unrelated zero-share portfolio would count tax unrelated to the covered home.

For policy `p`, whole-home valuation `F_p(V)` is calculated once at property value `V` using policy `p`'s tiers. For **each** row, compute `N_p,bju(V) = s_bju F_p(V) + A_bju − D_bju`, then `T_p,bju(V) = tax_p(N_p, m_bju)` via the existing bounded [calculator rules](wealth_tax_supported_scenarios.md#supported-inputs-and-order-of-calculation). Do not tax the sum of separate co-owners' wealth, divide the final full-owner tax by their shares, or give each co-owner a fresh whole-property 14m valuation band. Assessment flag is an asserted eligibility scenario, not a legal classification inferred from household type.

Let `G_p,bj(V) = Σ_u T_p,bju(V)`. Under uniform within-bin prices, the illustrative *annual model total* for policy `p` is

`M_p = Σ_b n_b Σ_j q_bj [1/(U_b−L_b) ∫_(L_b)^(U_b) G_p,bj(V) dV]`.

The reform difference is `Δ = M_reform − M_reference` (positive = more tax in this model). Each property has total mixture weight `n_b`, **not** `n_b` per co-owner. For implementation, a row's tax-calculation multiplicity is `n_b q_bj` per property template, never `n_b q_bj s_bju`; shares allocate housing *value*, not taxpayer counts. `Σ_j q_bj` and shares are separately normalised; do not normalise tax-unit row weights to one. The implied model tax-unit count is `Σ_b n_b Σ_j q_bj (# rows in j)`; it can exceed the property count without double-counting a property. This number is assumption-dependent, not a published national count. No allowance is shared across separate units or duplicated within a joint unit.

## Explicit illustrative starting design for T3b to evaluate, not a default claim

This small example demonstrates a **property-conditional** mixture; all probabilities, holdings and shares here are assumptions. Group existing 1m bins into <14m, 14–30m; retain their original counts/edges for integration, and treat the assumed >30m tail separately. Apply the group probabilities to each constituent bin without altering its count. Amounts below are **million NOK per unit**; within a group, hold them fixed along each bin, not at the group's average property value.

| Bin group | One individual full owner `F` | One qualifying joint full-owner unit `J` | Two separate 50/50 individual units `S` | `F` assets/debt | `J` assets/debt | `S` assets/debt **per unit** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| <14m | 0.80 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0 / 1.6 | 0 / 1.6 | 0 / 0.8 |
| 14–30m | 0.60 | 0.20 | 0.20 | 0.5 / 2 | 0.5 / 2 | 0.25 / 1 |
| >30m assumed tail | 0.50 | 0.25 | 0.25 | 2 / 3 | 2 / 3 | 1 / 1.5 |

Each row's three probabilities sum to 1 **per property bin**; the numbers do not describe observed tenure, marriage or debt levels. In `S`, the two identical rows each get one individual allowance: a property selecting `S` generates two tax-unit calculations, not two properties. Equal shares are illustrative only; test a 25/75 separate-owner alternative with independently allocated `A,D` (do not halve or quarter the tax or duplicate assets/debt). The `J` and `S` columns deliberately hold equal *property-level* total `A,D` within a group so that changing assessment/ownership structure can be isolated from changing aggregate holdings. `F` versus `J` likewise isolates allowance/band status in this example. None of those households can be inferred by joining SSB means to the histogram.

These are the T3b helper's illustrative inputs, **not a displayed or empirically justified UI default**. If later tasks select different illustrative inputs, document and test each change before presenting results. Validate finite nonnegative counts/amounts/probabilities, valid bin widths/nonoverlap, template share sum, supported unit types and the tail cap before calculation; reject unsupported legal cases instead of silently weighting them as zero.

## Baseline, integration and sensitivity

Keep the **legacy full-owner common-profile** result visible and numerically unchanged: `weighted_policy_effect` uses selected debt/assets/assessment for one unit at `s=1` on *each* property, ignores the personal share slider, and applies the same assumed tail bins. For an apples-to-apples engine regression, set all `q_b,F=1`, use the same tax-unit inputs and tail as the legacy calculation, and compare reform-minus-reference uniform estimates and conditional within-bin extrema. The illustrative mixture above is a **separate** scenario; its differences cannot be attributed solely to ownership if its balance sheets, template weights or tail also changed. Display both policies' model totals and their difference for a fixed scenario; do not describe a difference between differently populated baselines as a policy effect.

For fixed `A,D,s,m`, the supported tiered valuation and tax schedules are continuous piecewise linear in whole-home `V`. Retain exact integration by splitting each *original* bin at its edges and **both policies'** whole-home valuation limits and unit-specific allowance/upper-band crossing values (including zero share only in standalone tests). Sum trapezoid areas on those linear segments; multiply by `n_b q_bj` **after** integrating the per-template sum of unit taxes. The existing helper's minimum/maximum are extrema of the *difference* at split points per bin, conditional on arbitrary placement of the bin's properties with fixed profiles; for templates, apply the same criterion to `G_reform,bj − G_reference,bj` before weighting. They are not joint demographic bounds, and bins' independently selected extremal locations need not describe an observed price distribution. For baseline and reform totals use the same uniform distribution and profiles.

No numerical integration is required for fixed-profile T3b. If later introducing `A,D` or template probabilities varying continuously with `V`, the old trapezoids are no longer necessarily exact: explicitly declare the new price/profiles distribution, compare its quadrature against exact fixed-profile/identity/constant/linear and kinked cases, subdivide at all known breakpoints, and report convergence on successively finer grids (e.g. N, 2N, 4N) with an explicit NOK tolerance and stable sign/totals before using it in the UI. A sampled chart grid alone is not an integration proof.

Show scenario *sensitivity*, not a statistical interval: vary the assumed >30m count/cap, bins' within-bin placement, probabilities of `F/J/S`, 25/75 versus 50/50 shares, and allocations of debt/other assets **conditional on price and ownership**, including a high-price/high-other-assets versus high-price/high-debt contrast. For a clean correlation comparison, hold property counts, price-bin assignments, total assumed assets/debt and unit-type mix fixed while exchanging portfolios across price groups (or report exactly which marginals change); evaluate both policies on each identical assumed population. Where assumptions change together, label the result a combined scenario rather than an isolated effect. Parameter ranges are user-chosen examples, **not** empirical percentiles, low/high credible bounds or confidence intervals; order result labels by computed values, not by presumed fiscal direction. Counts above the tail cap, unknown source-bin edges/digitisation, unobserved joint correlations, legal exclusions, behavioural changes and actual tax payments are outside any reported range.

T3b acceptance checks should include `Σ_j q_bj=1`, property-count conservation, expected tax-unit multiplicity, no repeated joint allowance, two independent owners versus one joint unit, policy-identity zero change, deterministic additive bin weights, and collapse to `weighted_policy_effect` for the one-full-owner common-profile case (including bin extrema). T3c must keep official figures and 2024 reference views static and separate from this assumption-driven calculation. T3b's engine has no dashboard callers yet; T3c must verify its browser behaviour before displaying results.
